# Tile 7: Logistics Transition Zone

## Description
Late-stage urban district transitioning toward logistics/service dominance between the dense core and the fully industrial periphery.
Large resident population remains, but life is compressed and subordinated to circulation + utilities.

## Dryness (hard)
- Tile 7 must remain **dry**.
- No visible river, coastline, harbor, bay, canal, or distant open-water horizon read.
- Do not allow far-background blue bands or reflective planes that read as water.

**Not suburbia.**

## Role
- Threshold between dense urban life and industrial systems
- Shift from residential/commercial priority → logistics-driven urban form
- Prepares transition toward Tile 9

## Airport detail enrichment (optional, secondary)
- A small control/observation tower is encouraged as a secondary airport detail.
- A few small hangars or service sheds may appear occasionally, but must remain subordinate to the embedded-airport composition.
- One aircraft may occasionally appear taxiing on the main runway, or in a believable takeoff/landing posture, but only if scale and realism remain convincing.
- These are enrichment details, not the main subject.

## Density & Form
- High density via **large footprints + infrastructure layering**
- Predominantly low-rise to mid-rise; repeated masses (**no vertical landmarks**)
- Compressed, aged, service-oriented fabric

## Key Elements
- Large repetitive residential blocks (working-class; non-iconic)
- Residential blocks interwoven with infrastructure (roads/ramps cutting through clusters)
- Broad load-bearing roads with layered intersections
- Multi-level circulation: surface streets + flyovers + ramps/partial interchanges; **occasional tunnels**
- Warehouses/service slabs; embedded utilities (pipelines, water backbone, service corridors)
- Vehicle variety emphasized per Core Canonical Vehicles (Tiles 7–9 heavy logistics mix)

## Anti–Tile 5 Bleed (hard)
- The far background must **not** contain an iconic CBD cluster or recognizable “core skyline crest.”
- **Ban:** tall clustered supertalls, art-deco spires, Manhattan-like hero skyline, any centered skyline hump/crest.
- **Replace with:** flattened distant urban haze + logistics sprawl + low industrial silhouettes + distant low ridgelines; background is non-hero, low-verticality, low-contrast.
- If any distant “city center” exists at all, it may appear only as a **tiny indistinct low silhouette** pushed to the **far-left third** (never centered), heavily diffused, with no readable landmark shapes.

## Airport (canonical, Tile 7 only)
- **Urban airport/tarmac DECK** embedded into city fabric (Congonhas/LAX-under-runway vibe), **far-midground**
- Partial runway/tarmac segment only (no full runway edge-to-edge; no hero terminal; no readable markings)
- **Layered deck (hard):** runway/tarmac is elevated on columns/structure; underside beams/columns are visible and cast a deck shadow onto roads/city beneath
- **Under-tarmac tunnels/underpasses:** multiple avenues pass beneath (>=2 visible tunnel mouths / under-runway road cuts)
- **Vanishing-point blend (hard):** tarmac edges must taper to the same global vanishing point as roads/roof-fields (no horizon-parallel "flat bar")
- **Anti-zoom (hard):** airport must NOT change camera/FOV/altitude; if runway wants legibility via push-in, shrink it and keep it farther
- **No-hero legibility (hard):** airport details must be only lightly readable at this distance; if it becomes crisp/hero, the framing is too tight → pull back

### Seam strip lock (hard, 1024×1536)
- Leftmost **120px** and rightmost **120px** are **seam blending strips**: low-uniqueness repeatable texture only.
- **Forbidden in seam strips:** runway/deck edges, terminal edges, taxiway paint/lines, aircraft, jet bridges, cranes, bridges/viaducts, rail lines, or any long straight high-contrast line.
- Airport deck/runway must remain inside **x = 160..864** (never near side edges).
- If a deck/runway edge trends toward a side, it must terminate/occlude/step down into buildings **well before** the seam strip.

### Airport life (required, proportional)
- 3–7 aircraft total, mixed positions (some parked at gates, one taxiing/holding)
- Jet bridges visible for at least 1–3 parked aircraft
- Small control/observation tower (non-hero)
- Small ground support vehicles (baggage carts, belt loaders, fuel truck, pushback tug, service vans)
- **Aircraft randomization:** avoid repeating the same aircraft model; mix different silhouettes/sizes; use fake/artistic liveries (abstract colors/patterns; no readable airline names/logos)

## Visibility (anti-fog)
- No fog veil / no milky smog blur
- Distance rolloff allowed only as subtle desaturation/contrast reduction; edges remain crisp

## Forbidden Motifs
- Suburban calm; clean master-planned order; isolated neighborhoods
- Iconic skylines / landmark towers / centered CBD crest
- River/coastline/harbor/canal/open-water read
- Full industrial dominance (ports/refineries/container megayards belong to Tile 9)
- Large empty runway shoulder or open apron field leaking into the left seam toward Tile 6

## Calibration Notes
- Follow Tile 7 **R1 composition map** (when provided) + _docs/02_tile_system.md → Framing Stability Protocol_
- Avoid near-foreground interchange dominance; increase midground stacking/overlap
- Light-direction rule (Tiles 6–9): key from **left**, shadows fall **right**
- **Wide framing bias (anti-zoom):** prefer a farther/higher framing where elements read smaller and more surrounding city context is visible; airport remains far-midground and does not dominate the frame
- **Scale parity (Tile 5 ruler):** match Tile 5 apparent pixel scale for buildings/vehicles; if Tile 7 reads larger, it is zoomed in → pull back

## Zoom helper pass (rare fallback)
Use only if Tile 7 still reads zoomed-in after applying the wide-framing + scale parity rules:
> Keep the image identical (same perspective, horizon, lighting, airport deck geometry).
> Reframe wider / zoom out: expand the scene so everything becomes smaller in the frame and more surrounding city is visible.
> Do not change the layout; only reveal more area around it (more foreground and side context). No new focal landmarks.
