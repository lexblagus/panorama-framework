# Tile 1: Nature as Dominant Chaos

## General Tile Identity
- Tile Name: Tile 1
- Tile Type: Primary
- Role: The natural extreme of the panorama.
- Primary Visuals: rugged Mediterranean / Italian-style coastline wedge, terrain-dominant and vertically aggressive.

## Core Identity
- Tile 1 is expressed as a rugged **coastline wedge** ("/", not "V").
- RIGHT side is dominant cliff / hillside landmass rising to the RIGHT.
- LEFT side is open sea / inlet negative space.
- The scene must feel non-urban, infrastructurally scarred but terrain-dominant.

## Density and Form
- Density: Very low (non-urban)
- Form: Terrain-dominant, vertically aggressive
- Composition lock: envelope slope rises toward the RIGHT
- Dominant diagonal read: bottom-right → top-left (when R1 exists, it is the authority)

## Terrain Envelope / Sky Preservation (hard)
- The RIGHT hillside must remain dominant, but it must read as a **receding coastal slope**, not as a towering mountain wall occupying the upper-right corner.
- Preserve a meaningful upper-right sky wedge.
- The hillside / ridgeline should generally remain **under the skyline envelope / sky budget discipline** and should not climb excessively into the top-right sky.
- Avoid terrain mass touching or nearly touching the top frame edge on the RIGHT.
- The far-right landmass may feel high and dominant, but it should taper into distance rather than rising into a vertical wall.
- Solve dominance through slope mass, rock, roads, and settlements — not by letting the hill consume the sky.

## Atmosphere and Lighting
- Atmosphere: clear, crisp daylight; strong material definition; respects lateral gradient
- Lighting target: key light from FAR RIGHT; cast shadows fall LEFT (best-effort; do not destabilize framing to “fix” it)
- No sun disk

## Key Elements
- Steep cliffs, exposed rock, rugged elevation changes (RIGHT)
- Coastal river / stream + narrow estuary / inlet / reservoir meeting the sea **without** forming a centered V-valley
- Cascades / waterfall / spillway
- Terrain should feel stepped / receding / terrace-like in depth, rather than one giant continuous mountain shoulder.
- Optional cliff-integrated dam / reservoir step (visible controlled drop)
- Dense irregular vegetation variety (Mediterranean shrubs / pines + mixed hillside growth)

## Infrastructure
- Infrastructure must read as cuts / scars, not urban grid.
- Show a **stacked coastal road system** carved into rock:
  - lower coastline road
  - mid-slope contour road
  - occasional upper viaduct fragment
- Show 2–4 tunnel mouths at different heights, short viaduct spans, retaining walls, and switchbacks.
- Roads should be partially occluded by vegetation / rock; avoid one single clean highway ribbon.
- Optional passenger rail cue (subordinate): short hillside segment or tunnel mouth + brief viaduct hugging terrain (not a hero corridor)
- Optional funicular / teleférico cue (one only): small, realistic scale, subordinate detail
- Upper-slope roads / settlements should not force the terrain to rise too high into the sky.

## Settlements
- More hillside settlements (still non-urban): terraced village pockets + hillside houses + a few mansions / villas
- Interconnection cues: stairways, footpaths, small pedestrian bridges, terraces, retaining walls, switchback alleys

## Secondary Detail
- Caves / grottoes encouraged: sea caves / cliff cavities as secondary detail
- Road read: coastline road is a major leading line entering near lower-right and receding toward top-left

## Seam Strip Lock (for 1024×1536)
- Leftmost 120px and rightmost 120px are seam blending strips.
- LEFT seam: quiet open water only.
- RIGHT seam: repeatable rock / vegetation + small non-unique buildings only.
- No edge-clipped hero objects.
- No large linear element touching side edges.
