> UPLOADS / INPUTS:

>
> REFERENCE USE POLICY (STRICT):
> - Transfer ONLY from Reference A: camera/framing physics (horizon/vanishing-height read), rectilinear telephoto compression feel, vertical “pressure”, sky-budget discipline, and midground scale.
> - Transfer FORBIDDEN from Reference A: any Tile 5 content identity (no landmark silhouettes, no “core” signature shapes).
>
> GENERATOR PROMPT:
> Generate Tile 7: Logistics Transition Zone. Photorealistic aerial megacity slice in portrait orientation. A late-stage urban district transitioning toward logistics/service dominance while still supporting a large resident population. This is NOT suburbia and NOT a clean master-planned city.
>
> CAMERA / OPTICS (MUST MATCH THE RULER):
> - Fixed elevated aerial viewpoint; rectilinear optics; strong telephoto-like depth compression; narrow field of view.
> - Maintain pitch/framing stability relative to the ruler (do not vertically recenter).
> - If elements pressure the top, crop/clip them; do NOT open more sky.
> - Avoid push-in reads: do not let a single near-foreground interchange fill the bottom; increase depth via midground stacking and overlap.
>
> SPATIAL ENERGY (DIRECTIONAL CONTINUITY):
> - Dominant spatial energy reads as diagonal bottom-left → top-right through mass orientation and layered circulation (not cinematic tilt).
>
> PRIMARY MASSING (RESIDENTIAL + SERVICE DOMINANCE):
> - Large, repetitive residential blocks (working-class character), low-rise to mid-rise, aggregated into big footprints.
> - Housing reads as population support, not neighborhood identity: compressed, aged, negotiated, wrapped around circulation/utility corridors.
> - Residential blocks interwoven with infrastructure: ramps cut through clusters; buildings hug flyovers and service corridors.
>
> CIRCULATION (LAYERED, LOAD-BEARING):
> - Broad, load-bearing roads with layered intersections, partial interchanges, flyovers, ramps, occasional tunnel mouths.
> - Multi-level circulation: surface streets + elevated decks + ramps; telephoto-compressed stacking into depth.
>
> EARLY LOGISTICS (PRESENT BUT NOT DOMINANT):
> - Service traffic diversity > volume:
>   - rigid multi-axle trucks, cab-over and non-cab-over
>   - articulated semi-trailers
>   - fuel/gas tanker trucks (few)
>   - canvas-tarp truck bodies in muted orange/brown (some new, some weathered)
>
> HI-TECH / “OPEN-STRUCTURE” TECH NODES (ADD THESE, EMBEDDED IN THE FABRIC):
> - Fleet electrification yard embedded among housing + warehouses:
>   - EV bus/truck charging lanes with tall charging masts, cable trays, transformer skids, protective bollards
>   - canopies and gantries (industrial, utilitarian), NOT a clean futuristic plaza
> - Rail/metro maintenance & inspection depot:
>   - open-sided maintenance sheds, inspection pits, overhead crane rails, catenary gantries
>   - short rail spurs and switching ladders feeding the depot (not a container terminal)
> - Automated parcel / urban logistics spine:
>   - exterior conveyor galleries bridging between low-rise slabs across service roads
>   - sorting structures visible through open bays (avoid a single giant sealed box)
> - Grid + telecom complexity pockets:
>   - dense substation/switchyard nodes tucked into the fabric (lattice frames, busbars, transformers)
>   - antenna arrays / microwave towers / radome-like elements (small, scattered; not a landmark)
> - Construction-tech yard (optional but helpful for visual variety):
>   - precast concrete fabrication with open rebar forests, formwork racks, crane rails
>   - pipe-spool staging yards (visible piping) WITHOUT petrochemical towers

> IMPORTANT “NOT BOX FACTORY” RULE:
> - Do NOT create one huge clean boxy mega-factory as the hero. Prefer many interlocked open-sided sheds, gantries, cable trays, and exterior process-like service structures embedded among housing blocks and roads.
>
> WHAT MUST REMAIN ABSENT (DO NOT DRIFT INTO TILE 8/9):

> - No petrochemical tower forests, no flare stacks, no heavy smokestack dominance.

> - Brightest haze/sky pressure should sit on the LEFT side, not the right.
> - Wind pushes smoke/steam/dust to the RIGHT (keep emissions subtle and low-contrast).
