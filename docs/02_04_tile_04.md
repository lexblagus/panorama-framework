# Tile 4 Spec: Pre-Core Urban Consolidation Belt

## General Tile Identity
- Tile Name: Tile 4
- Tile Type: Tertiary
- Role: Transitional pre-core urban belt between Tile 3’s greener residential fabric and Tile 5’s ultra-dense metropolitan core.
- Primary Visuals: Dense urban consolidation, rising verticality, civic-transit complexity, and dry utility infrastructure embedded into the city.
- Core Read: A city becoming decisively metropolitan — less residential and garden-like than Tile 3, less iconic and tower-dominant than Tile 5, but clearly preparing for the ruler-core.

## Core Identity
- Tile 4 is a dense pre-core district.
- It must feel urban, dry, infrastructural, and increasingly vertical.
- It is not a landmark postcard tile.
- It is not a logistics or industrial tile.
- It is not a park tile.
- It is not a waterfront tile.
- Its identity comes from distributed systems and repeated urban motifs, not from one single massive hero object.

## Density and Form
- High density.
- Mixed mid-rise to high-rise urban massing.
- Clear vertical escalation from left to right:
  - left side still carries some Tile 3 fabric: denser residential blocks, mixed-use streets, interwoven greenery, and human-scale texture
  - center becomes more consolidated, infrastructural, civic, and transit-heavy
- right side rises toward Tile 5 with taller towers, stronger vertical pressure, tighter block packing, and more intense skyline buildup
- Tile 4 must not read as low or flat overall.
- Even when using repeated civic/institutional motifs, the district must retain strong metropolitan compression.
- **Skyline envelope (hard):**
  - Tile 4 must NOT form a centered skyline hump, mini-core crest, or near-symmetric inverted-V.
  - Background and far-midground built mass must rise progressively from LEFT to RIGHT.
  - Highest skyline pressure belongs in the RIGHT third, feeding Tile 5.
  - The center may densify, but must not become the dominant skyline apex.
  - The envelope must read as a sustained climb / ramp, not as a broad arch whose apex merely drifts rightward.
  - Avoid any “^^^” behavior across Tiles 4–5–6; Tile 4 is the build-up tile, not the first of three crowns.

## Dry Urban Band (hard)
- Tile 4 must remain dry.
- No visible river, coastline, harbor, bay, canal, lake, or open-water read.
- No distant blue water band at the horizon.
- No reflective plane may read as water.
- Water may appear only as infrastructure:
  - exposed utility pipelines
  - risers
  - valve yards
  - service conduits
  - buried runs emerging from and disappearing into the ground
  - water towers
- No scenic water bodies.

## Signature District Logic
Tile 4 should read as a distributed civic-transit-utility district.

This means:
- repeated urban systems
- multiple institutional anchors
- multiple transit fragments
- multiple utility-water structures
- no single privileged centerpiece dominating the whole frame

## Key Repeated Motifs

### 1. Large Urban Water Pipelines
- Large exposed utility-water pipelines are a recurring Tile 4 signature.
- These are not domestic pipes and not industrial refinery pipes.
- They are major urban utility conduits:
  - thick as real large water mains can get
  - substantial enough to be legible at aerial scale
  - smaller than buildings
  - visually embedded into streets, corridors, rail cuts, retaining walls, and block edges
- Pipelines should appear as **multiple distributed episodes**, not one single giant hero ribbon controlling the whole frame.
- Prefer 2–4 legible pipeline moments across different depths / blocks.
- Pipelines may:
  - run overhead on supports
  - cross above roads
  - emerge from underground and re-enter the ground
  - connect to pumping or service structures
  - form short elevated crossings between blocks
- Some segments may disappear behind buildings or terrain cuts before re-emerging.
- Pipelines must feel infrastructural and urban, not fantastical or industrial-megaplant in scale.

### 2. Water Towers
- Include multiple water towers distributed through the district.
- Use varied typologies:
  - cylindrical towers
  - skeletal towers
  - elevated tanks
  - modernist towers
  - V-shaped or bifurcated support designs
  - rooftop water tanks on larger buildings
- Water towers should be integrated into the urban skyline as repeated infrastructural landmarks, not isolated rural objects.
- They help define Tile 4 identity and should appear across different depths of the scene.

### 3. Civic and Institutional Buildings
- Include multiple civic/institutional structures spread through the district:
  - hospitals
  - schools
  - municipal buildings
  - market halls
  - museums
  - libraries
  - courts
  - universities or technical campuses
- These must be urban and multi-floor, not low suburban campus buildings.
- They should feel embedded into a dense city, with heavier footprints, rooftop clutter, service access, and layered adjacency to streets and transit.
- Some may use older monumental styles:
  - neo-gothic
  - beaux-arts
  - art-deco
  - civic monumental masonry
- Do not let any one institutional building become a singular postcard landmark.

### 4. Transit Fragments
- Transit should appear as distributed fragments rather than one giant terminal.
- Allowed elements:
  - elevated rail segments
  - platform canopies
  - concourse roofs
  - pedestrian overpasses
  - stair towers
  - rail trench edges
  - short viaduct fragments
  - station entrances
  - under-track passages
- Transit must feel urban and embedded, not like a clean isolated transport megaproject.

### 5. Sports / Recreation Fragments (optional secondary motif)
- Sports infrastructure may appear, but only as secondary distributed traces:
  - partial arena edge
  - training grounds
  - roof courts
  - school athletic yards
  - bleacher fragments
- Do not make Tile 4 “the stadium tile.”
- A full heroic stadium should be avoided.

### 6. Antennas and Rooftop Telecom Texture
- Add antennas and telecom clutter across the district.
- Especially on taller civic, office, residential, and institutional buildings.
- Think dense urban rooftop utility language:
  - Paulista-like telecom crowns
  - masts
  - dishes
  - relay frames
  - rooftop mechanical clutter
  - water tanks
  - utility penthouses
- These elements should help densify the skyline and add realism.

## Building Character
- Mixed-use urban blocks dominate.
- Buildings should feel older, layered, and continuously modified.
- Architectural diversity is important:
  - brick and stone mid-rises
  - concrete apartment slabs
  - office towers
  - older monumental institutional buildings
  - glass-and-concrete commercial towers
  - dense podiums and rooftop clutter
- Right side must begin approaching Tile 5’s intensity:
  - taller towers
  - more frequent high-rise interruptions
  - less greenery
  - tighter massing
  - stronger vertical pressure

## Greenery
- Greenery is allowed, but reduced compared to Tile 3.
- It should appear as:
  - medians
  - plaza trees
  - institutional courtyards
  - residual green pockets
  - rooftop planting
- No large scenic park.
- No lush residential-green dominance.
- Tile 4 must read as urban first.

## Roads and Circulation
- Layered road hierarchy:
  - local streets
  - broader avenues
  - short overpasses
  - underpasses
  - ramps
  - service lanes
- Avoid one single giant boulevard dominating the tile.
- Movement should feel braided and distributed.
- Infrastructure should be interlaced with blocks rather than isolated.

## Height Progression (hard)
- Tile 4 must preserve the left-to-right build-up toward Tile 5.
- Left side:
  - denser than Tile 3
  - still mixed and somewhat human-readable
- Center:
  - consolidated mid-rise and rising civic/institutional/transit density
- Right side:
  - significantly taller and more compressed
  - early tower field feeding into Tile 5
- Tile 4 must not visually “drop” before Tile 5.
- The skyline must prepare the eye for Tile 5’s ruler-core.
- **Do not let the center become a secondary core-like crest.**
- The right third, not the center, carries the strongest background tower pressure.

## Horizon and Sky
- Horizon remains dry and urban.
- No visible sun disk.
- Light direction should follow the current canon for this part of the panorama.
- Sky budget must remain controlled.
- No excessive open sky.
- Horizon line should remain broadly stable relative to neighboring tiles; solve the transition through the skyline envelope, not by creating a dramatic horizon jump.

## Seam and Stitching Logic

### Left seam toward Tile 3
- Must remain compatible with Tile 3’s denser residential urban fabric.
- Allow some greenery traces, smaller blocks, and lower urban texture frequency.
- Do not introduce sudden tower jumps directly on the seam.

### Right seam toward Tile 5
- Must prepare for Tile 5’s ultra-dense core.
- Increase vertical pressure and massing toward the right.
- Keep seam strips free of singular landmark objects.
- Use repeatable dense urban texture and early tower rhythm.
- The seam should feel like a rising pre-core ramp into Tile 5, not like a separate centered crest beside Tile 5.

### Seam Strip Lock (for 1024×1536)
- Leftmost 120px and rightmost 120px are seam blending strips.
- These strips must contain low-uniqueness repeatable urban texture only.
- Forbidden in seam strips:
  - full institutional hero facades
  - dominant water towers
  - giant pipeline crossings ending at the edge
  - stadium bowls
  - strong diagonals
  - isolated tower icons
  - open-water reads
  - any centered skyline-crest behavior

## Forbidden Motifs
- River, coast, harbor, canal, lake, or open-water read
- Ships, port cranes, dock geometry
- Full industrial dominance
- Logistics yards
- Refinery elements
- Giant singular stadium hero
- Nature-dominant composition
- Rural or suburban feel
- Single iconic landmark dominating the tile
- Overly sterile office district uniformity

## Additional Notes
- Tile 4 should feel like the city’s infrastructural metabolism becoming visible.
- Water is present only as utility infrastructure, never as landscape.
- Identity must come from distributed systems, not one object.
- The tile should feel increasingly compressed, urban, and metropolitan as it approaches Tile 5.
