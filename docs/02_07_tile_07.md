# Tile 7: Logistics Transition Zone

## Canonical Human Spec

### Description

Tile 7 represents a **late-stage urban district transitioning toward logistics and service dominance**, situated between the dense urban core and the fully industrial periphery. The city supports a **large resident population**, but residential life is increasingly compressed and subordinated to circulation, commerce, and utility systems. This is **not suburbia** and not a residential neighborhood.

The city feels older, heavier, and negotiated, shaped by throughput rather than comfort.

### Role

- Acts as the **threshold between dense urban life and industrial systems**
- Signals the shift from residential/commercial priority to **logistics-driven urban form**
- Introduces population mass without urban centrality
- Prepares the perceptual and semantic transition toward Tile 9

### Density & Form

- High density via **large-scale aggregation and infrastructure layering**
- Predominantly low-rise to mid-rise construction
- Emphasis on **large footprints and repeated masses**, not vertical landmarks
- Compressed, aged, service-oriented fabric

### Key Elements

- **Large residential blocks** (working-class; repetitive; non-iconic)
- Residential blocks interwoven with infrastructure (roads/ramps cutting through clusters; buildings wrapped around circulation/utilities)
- Broad load-bearing roads with layered intersections
- Multi-level circulation (surface streets, flyovers, ramps, partial interchanges, occasional tunnels)
- Early logistics/service traffic (present but not dominant): rigid multi-axle trucks, cargo vehicles, articulated trucks, tankers
- Low-rise commercial slabs, warehouses, service buildings
- Embedded utilities: pipelines, water backbone, service corridors

### Forbidden Motifs

- Suburban patterns; landscaped housing calm
- Iconic skylines / landmark architecture
- Full industrial systems (ports, refineries, container yards)
- Clean master-planned order; pedestrian-centric design
- Isolated residential neighborhoods

## Prompt Core (Invariant)

- Photorealistic aerial perspective; portrait orientation
- Fixed elevated camera position; rectilinear optics
- Strong telephoto-like depth compression; unified camera system
- Golden hour lighting; sun not visible in frame
- No wide-angle distortion; no stylistic filters

## Active Calibration Layer (Anchored)

- Residential mass must be **large-scale and repetitive** (population support, not neighborhood identity)
- Logistics/service systems drive spatial organization (without full industrial dominance)
- Vehicle diversity preferred over vehicle volume
- **Directional continuity (post-core release):** diagonal energy **bottom-left → top-right** (via mass orientation + circulation layering, not cinematic tilt)
- **Framing + scale discipline:** avoid a single near-foreground interchange filling the bottom; increase **midground stacking** (residential slabs + warehouses + service yards overlapping in layers)
- **Light-direction enforcement for Tiles 6–9:** key light from the **left**; primary cast shadows fall to the **right**
